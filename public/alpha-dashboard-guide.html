01 — Orientation

What this dashboard is

It answers one question: is the AI Profit Map ready to open to the public?

Every number on it exists to move that verdict, or to tell someone what to fix so it moves. If a number does neither, it doesn't belong.

It is not a usage or growth dashboard. It measures whether an alpha product has become a real one — and it is built to refuse to lie when the data is thin or broken.

The principle running through everything: a number that can't be trusted must never look like one that can. Every value carries its sample size and a confidence dot. Always. No exceptions.

The passive layer is the most valuable part of this system. When a tester corrects a volume in the validation wizard, they are telling us our model was wrong, and by exactly how much. We record it automatically. They never know they gave feedback.

03 — Architecture

The pipeline

alpha_feedback is RLS-locked, service-role only. Nothing reads or writes it from the browser. All writes go through /api/alpha/progress; all reads through /api/alpha/dashboard.

Why it's built this way

The old table was created by hand in the Supabase UI, with no migration. The code drifted from the schema — it wrote columns that didn't exist — and Postgres silently rejected every row containing them. The PMF survey discarded every response for weeks. Nobody noticed, because nothing ever threw.

Rules that exist because of that bug, and must not be relaxed: the schema lives in a migration file, never in the UI. Writes go through a server route that fails loudly. Every funnel flag is written independently, so one failing write can never mask another.

Identity

A tester is real only if they arrived through a live invite. The chain: alpha_invites → user_metadata.invite_id → alpha_feedback.invite_id, joined to reports for the company name. Internal @lyrise.ai testing is filtered out and never counted.

All four must pass. They are the entire point of the dashboard.

05 — The rule

Confidence

Reliable n ≥ 20
Building n ≥ 5
Low n < 5

A number never appears without its dot and its n. The old dashboard showed "PMF: 100%" from three responses — technically correct, completely misleading. A percentage from three people looks exactly as authoritative as one from three hundred. The chip makes it impossible to read the number without also reading how much to trust it.

PMF is always shown, even at n=3 — but always chipped and caveated. Showing a number and passing a gate are two different things.

06 — Reference

Every metric

Model accuracy — the most valuable numbers here

MetricHealthyWhat bad means
Workflows kepthighWe're proposing work that doesn't exist at that company.
Volume driftnear 0%Negative = we overestimate how often a workflow runs. Positive = we underestimate.
Duration driftnear 0%The same, for minutes-per-item.
Workflows added0Our research is blind to entire categories of work. Unit: workflows, not testers.
Workflows removed0The ones we should stop proposing.

Drift is a mean of signed values. An overestimate on one workflow and an underestimate on another cancel out. It tells you our bias, not our error size. A drift of 0% could mean we're perfect — or wildly wrong in both directions.

Why some reports are excluded: reports made before baseline tracking existed have no record of the AI's original guess. Their "baseline" would be the tester's already-corrected numbers, so drift against it would read as zero — a silent lie. They're excluded, and the count is shown.

Trust delta

Trust before → after, either side of the validation wizard. The wizard exists to turn "a starting guess" into "the truth." The delta is the entire proof it works. Negative means validation is destroying confidence — the corrections needed were big enough to undermine the whole report.

Experience

Intake ease — is the form losing people before they see value? Report clarity — do they understand the output? Why it was unclear — asked only when clarity ≤ 3, and the most actionable thing on the dashboard: it names which part confuses people, rather than just that they are confused.

Recruiting target

// how many testers to go find this week
target = max(gap_a, gap_b)

gap_a = max(0, 20 − completed_surveys)   // sample floor
gap_b = 3 if last 3 testers raised new themes else 0

When this reaches zero and the gates pass, the alpha is done.

Purchase intent

This quarter / next quarter / exploring. A commercial signal, not product feedback. It goes to Marc. It never becomes a Linear ticket — routing sales intent to engineering is a category error.

07 — Behaviour

What the dashboard can detect

"What to fix" is generated, not hardcoded. Every metric has a healthy range; anything outside it becomes a ranked candidate.

SeverityDetected whenSurfaces as
1 — criticalFunnel counts inconsistent"A write is failing. Every number below is unreliable."
2 — criticalWorkflows kept < 80% · drift beyond ±25% · workflows added > 0"We propose work that doesn't exist" / "Our estimates are systematically off"
3 — seriousTrust delta < 0"Validation destroys confidence instead of building it"
4 — seriousA steep drop between two funnel steps"Testers abandon between X and Y"
5 — fixableClarity or intake ease < 3 · unclear-reason clusters"People don't understand X"
6 — strategicPMF < 40% once n ≥ 20"People don't need this enough"

Why this ordering. If the data is lying, nothing else on the page can be trusted — integrity first. If the model is wrong, no amount of UI polish saves the product — accuracy second. A beautiful, clear report full of invented numbers is worse than an ugly one full of true ones.

What it cannot detect

Anything nobody thought to measure. A tester who hates the pricing, wants a feature that doesn't exist, or found the tone patronising — none of that has a metric. That is what the open-text boxes are for, and why a human reads them every week. The system finds known failure modes; humans find unknown ones. No amount of cleverness removes that division of labour.

08 — Dynamics

The same dashboard, six stories

The verdict is synthesised from whichever gate is failing, ordered by severity. Same code, different message.

0 / 4"Funnel data is inconsistent — a write is failing."
Do: stop reading the dashboard. Fix the write. This exact failure produced the old "89% generated vs 78% intake" — impossible, and hidden for weeks.
1 / 4"Testers are removing 40% of the workflows we propose."
Do: fix the research pipeline, not the UI. The most serious failure mode there is — the core product is wrong, and no interface work saves it.
1 / 4"You need 17 more surveys before any score here can be trusted."
Do: recruit. Ignore every other number — they are noise until the sample clears. This is where the project stands today.
2 / 4"Validation is eroding trust rather than building it."
Do: the corrections testers make are large enough to destroy their confidence. That is a model problem wearing a UX mask.
3 / 4"Testers drop 12% between validation and report."
Do: look at that step. Usually UX, sometimes a bug.
4 / 4"Ready to ship."
Do: launch.
09 — Today

How thin data behaves

At n = 4: PMF bars are stubs. Most quote sections say "No responses yet." Model accuracy shows ~16 workflows across 4 reports — Building at best. The recruiting target dominates, because it should.

Do not act on any ⚪ Low-confidence number. It is displayed so you know it exists — not so you can decide from it. Low-confidence candidates can appear in "What to fix", but are never allowed to be the headline.

10 — Working on it

How to change it safely

Adding a question

  1. Add the column in a new migration file. Never edit an applied one. Never touch the Supabase UI.
  2. Add the field to the whitelist in /api/alpha/progress. Anything not whitelisted is silently dropped.
  3. Put the UI where the experience is fresh — not bundled into the end survey. Only 22% of testers ever reach the end.
  4. Add the metric to /api/alpha/dashboard with explains, formula, sample, caveat, healthy. All five. A metric without a sample size is a rumour.
  5. If it has a failure mode, add it to the what_to_fix candidates with a threshold and a severity — otherwise it will be invisible when it breaks.

Rules that are not negotiable

Never write to alpha_feedback from the clientRLS-locked. It will fail, and it will fail silently.
Never render a number without its sampleThe entire integrity of this dashboard rests on it.
Never hardcode an explanation in the UIThe route is the single source of truth for what every number means.
Never let a write fail quietlyLoud failure is the feature. Silence is the bug that cost weeks of PMF data.
11 — On purpose

What is not here, and why

Usage & growth metricsNot what an alpha measures.
A "demo data" toggleThe prototype had one. A real dashboard must never have a fake-data mode — someone will eventually mistake it for real.
Auto-created Linear ticketsFeedback becomes a ticket only after a human decides. Automation collects and ranks; humans judge. Without that gate, Linear fills with noise and the team stops reading it.
Word cloudsThey need hundreds of responses to mean anything. We will never have that.
A dashboard tourTooltips explain at the moment of confusion, which is when explanation actually lands. This document covers the rest.
12 — Twenty minutes

The weekly ritual

  1. Open What to fix. Read the top item. It is ranked by severity, so the top item genuinely is the most important thing.
  2. Decide: act / monitor / close. Only "act" becomes a Linear ticket. The button prefills it; a human reviews before creating it.
  3. Check the recruiting target. Give Marc the number and the persona.
  4. Glance at the verdict. If a gate changed, ask why.

That is the whole ritual. The dashboard's job is to make those four steps obvious. If it ever takes longer than twenty minutes, something on it has stopped earning its place — remove it.
